1 · The Problem & Industry Gap

Singapore lost S$1.1 billion to scams in 2024, with authorised push-payment (APP) scams — where the victim is logged in and authorises the transfer themselves — the fastest-growing category. (Singapore Police Force, Annual Scams & Cybercrime Brief 2024) Ordinary authentication cannot stop this: the customer is the one clicking confirm. Existing bank controls act mainly at the last step — the transfer itself — but the scam begins far earlier, and victims aged 50+ account for a disproportionate share of losses as Singapore's population ages toward 1 in 4 residents being seniors by 2030.

2 · Proposed Solution

Guardian Angel is an autonomous scam-defence layer for OCBC that reasons over a customer's caller identity, device behaviour, recipient network history and transaction pattern together — not the amount alone — to decide the least-intrusive protective action, and automatically opens a case for OCBC's fraud team when it acts. What makes it different: a deterministic, auditable rule engine computes risk; a bounded AI second opinion can only ever raise it, never lower it; and a policy engine — not the AI — is the sole enforcer of what happens to the customer's money. When staff confirm a case as a scam, that account is flagged for every future customer OCBC ever asks about it — the system gets smarter with every case, not just faster.

3 · How It Works — Three Interfaces, One Risk Engine

Customer App "/"
Grace's OCBC app — transfers, Verify Caller and Adaptive Care folded into one screen. Never shows a score; only a plain-language ask.
Staff Console "/staff"
OCBC risk-ops login → dashboard → case log → case investigation → Risk Account Registry. Where a human closes the loop.
Guardian Contact "/guardian"
The nominated guardian (e.g. an adult child) — informed, never in charge. Read-only alerts, no approval power over the account.
1Caller Verification
A live in-app code the caller must read back confirms a genuine authenticated OCBC session — stops impersonation before it starts.
2Recipient Network Check
Screens the recipient for mule-account patterns and, where a contact email is supplied, a real breach-exposure lookup.
3Smart-Lock (Money Lock)
Detects account-takeover sequences (new device + credential reset + limit change) and secures funds — reversible, never frozen.
4Adaptive Financial Care
Detects sustained manipulation over weeks and escalates proportionally: info → questions → cooling-off → guardian → human support.
The Risk Engine — Not One Signal, an Adaptive Combination

Every signal is tagged to a family (transaction, behavioural, device-security, recipient-network, identity). A diversity gate requires evidence from 2+ independent families before a high-risk action is allowed to stand — a large amount alone is never high risk. A bounded AI second opinion (Claude) can only raise the deterministic score, never lower it; a separate policy engine enforces the action and can never block funds directly — every decision reproducible, auditable and explainable.

R1 Account-Takeover — 2+ security changes + balance drain
R2 Scam-Recipient — large + new overseas payee + network flag
R3 Social-Engineering — new payee + savings liquidation + dismissed warnings
R4 Compromise + Mule — new device + network-flagged recipient
New — Risk Account Registry & the Staff Feedback Loop

Every transfer's recipient account is cross-referenced against a Risk Account Registry (Blacklisted / Watchlist / Cleared) — a real signal feeding the score, not a static list. When a case scores MEDIUM or above, it auto-opens in the Staff Console; when staff confirm it as a scam, the registry updates and every future transfer to that account is scored differently, for any customer.

6 · Financial & Timing Viability

Financial viability
  • Marginal cost per assessment ≈ 1–2 Claude API calls — cents, not dollars, per protected transfer.
  • No new customer-facing hardware or channel; runs inside OCBC's existing app and ops tooling.
  • A single prevented S$15,000 case (this build's own worked example) already covers years of API cost for that customer.
  • Registry-driven learning means detection cost per case falls as institutional memory grows — the opposite of a static rules list.
Timing viability
  • Now: working 3-interface prototype — User, Staff, Guardian — on a shared backend, demoed live.
  • Phase 1 (0–6m): pilot live scam interception with a limited risk-ops cohort.
  • Phase 2 (7–12m): vulnerability monitoring at scale + Great Eastern data synergies.
  • Phase 3 (Y2+): regional scaling of the registry as a shared industry signal.
